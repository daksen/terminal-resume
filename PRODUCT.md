# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Technical recruiters, engineering leads, and senior engineers evaluating a candidate. They open this on a laptop during a hiring review, scanning for signal: depth of experience, tech fluency, and personality. They're busy and skeptical. They've seen hundreds of portfolios. They need to feel, within seconds, that this person is the real deal.

## Product Purpose

Carlos Tavares's terminal-style portfolio resume. A Senior Software Engineer with 7+ years, specializing in React, Next.js, TypeScript, and frontend architecture. The site must communicate technical seriousness and individual voice simultaneously. Success: recruiter bookmarks it and moves Carlos to the next round.

## Brand Personality

Confident, precise, human.

The terminal aesthetic is not a gimmick — it signals that Carlos lives in this environment. Every design decision reinforces that: the interface IS the credential.

## Anti-references

- Generic HTML5UP/Bootstrap templates — the opposite of this
- SaaS landing pages: gradient text, hero metrics, infinite scroll animations
- PDF dumped on the web — no visual personality
- Over-engineered experimental portfolios that distract from the content

## Design Principles

1. **The interface is the credential.** The terminal theme earns trust directly. Every pixel must feel like it was written, not designed.
2. **Confidence over decoration.** No effects that draw attention to themselves. Let the content carry the weight.
3. **Human inside the machine.** Technical rigor + real personality (Padel, music, vibe coding). Don't sanitize it out.
4. **Scannability first.** Recruiters spend 30 seconds. Information hierarchy must reward that scan, not punish it.
5. **Nothing generic.** If a component could appear on any developer's portfolio, rethink it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG AA minimum (already enforced in CSS: `--color-terminal-muted` at 4.78:1 on bg)
- `prefers-reduced-motion` respected globally
- Semantic HTML with `sr-only` labels on sections
- Focus indicators via `--color-terminal-cyan` outline
